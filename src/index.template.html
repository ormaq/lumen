In Move mode, drag an object or its colored axis handles. Objects follow your cursor; Shift changes height. In Rotate mode, drag to turn yaw and tilt; X/Y/Z constrain rotation to a world axis. Enable snapping for 0.25-unit moves and 15° rotations. Touch: one finger edits or orbits; two fingers pan and pinch.

Copy, paste & targeting

Use Ctrl/⌘ C, X and V to copy, cut and paste objects. Shift+V pastes in place. Shift-click adds objects to the selection; Ctrl/⌘ A selects everything. Internal target locks are preserved when copying a group. Clipboard fallback works inside Lumen when browser clipboard access is unavailable. Text fields retain normal text-copy behavior.

In a source’s Emission section, “Aim once” sets a direction. “Lock onto” keeps the source aimed at the target as either moves. Release the target lock to edit direction freely. Transform locking is a separate control.

Stars, detectors & density

A Distant star is a circular wavefront sampled from infinity. Field X/Y are angular offsets from its base direction. Angular diameter samples a uniform apparent disk; it is not atmospheric seeing. The Analysis tab includes geometric, power-weighted focal-spot statistics, per-source centroids, optional Airy reference circles for an unobstructed circular aperture, and CSV hit export. Define mm per world unit to set the telescope scale.

Each source supports 16,384 rays; global density scales non-single sources up to that per-source ceiling, with 65,536 emitted samples and 100,000 segments per scene. Sources share the emitted-sample budget proportionally. Heavy traces run in a local worker when supported. The viewport can sample complete ray paths independently of the full analysis; its status shows any display thinning or trace truncation. Clear lines avoid additive whiteout; selecting a source emphasizes its path. Dim other sources, adjust line opacity, or switch to glow under View.

Built for the third dimension

Parabolic dishes have a point focus; troughs have a line focus. Curved mirrors use analytical intersections and surface normals rather than flat mesh approximations. Sources are independently colored and sampled in 3D. Splitters create two power-weighted branches. Detector power is the sum of sampled incident ray powers, in arbitrary units.

What this simulation means

This is a geometric-optics teaching tool, not a full optical-design solver. Mirrors are ideal and two-sided. Thin lenses use a paraxial approximation. A spherical mirror’s marked focus is paraxial, not an exact focus. Glass spheres are surrounded by air: overlapping or nested glass volumes are not supported. Fresnel reflection is optional per sphere; with it off, ordinary surface reflection loss is omitted, while total internal reflection still occurs.

Wavelength colors are a display approximation. RGB sources use 550 nm for glass dispersion. There is no diffraction, interference, polarization, wave-optical PSF, freeform glass, or physically calibrated color mixing. Grid, stands and emitter bodies are decorative, not optical obstacles. Ray ends are clipped by the path-length limit, energy threshold, interaction cap and performance budget. Positions span ±1,000,000 units on every axis. Far-away geometry uses adaptive camera clipping; small details at extreme coordinates may lose GPU precision. Animated dots illustrate direction, not the speed of light.

Inspired by PhyDemo Ray Optics Simulation. Reflection and refraction conventions follow Physically Based Rendering. Lumen is an independent implementation, not a full 3D port.

Geometric spot-diagram interpretation follows Ansys: What is a Point Spread Function? The Airy/Rayleigh reference is not a claim that this ray tracer resolves diffraction-limited stars.
